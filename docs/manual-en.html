Pegbar User Guide

1. Install

  1. Download Pegbar-1.0.1.ccx (or from the Pegbar page).
  2. Close Photoshop and double-click the .ccx. Creative Cloud installs it and warns that the plugin is not from the Marketplace: just confirm.
  3. Open Photoshop and go to Plugins > Pegbar by Coffee Cat > Pegbar. You can dock the panel under the canvas, like a timeline.

Requirements: Photoshop 2025 (version 26) or newer, on Windows or macOS.

To update: double-click the new version's .ccx; it replaces the previous one. Your files don't change.
Gray icon in Creative Cloud? That's normal for plugins installed from a file. Inside Photoshop the sheet icon shows up normally.

2. Getting started

How Pegbar stores your animation

  • An animation is a layer group whose name starts with [ANIM].
  • Each layer inside the group is one drawing (one sheet). The bottom layer is frame 1.
  • The exposure lives in the layer name: F003 [x2] is drawing 3 on 2s.
  • Key marks go at the end of the name: F003 [x2] {K}.

That's why your file opens normally even without the plugin: they're ordinary Photoshop layers.

Your first animation

  1. Click New animation group. The panel asks for a name (e.g. "Bouncing ball").
  2. Draw on frame 1 with Photoshop's brush.
  3. Click New frame (or press N) to get a new sheet. The onion skin shows the previous drawing in red.
  4. Repeat, then click Play to watch the animation.
Tip: keep the paper or background outside the [ANIM] group and draw on transparent layers. That way the onion skin color tints only the lines.

I already have drawings on layers

Select the layers in the Layers panel (or a group) and click Use the selected layers as an animation. They become frames, from bottom to top.

4. Timeline and frames

Navigate

first, previous, next, last. Or click a thumbnail in the timeline.

Create, duplicate, delete, move

ButtonWhat it does
New frameA new sheet right after the current frame, with the default exposure.
DuplicateA copy of the current frame right after it.
DeleteDeletes the selected frames (or the current one). Ctrl+Z undoes it.
MoveMoves the frame (or the selected ones) in the timeline.
Cut, copy and paste frames. Paste goes after the current frame.
Undo and redo: one step in Photoshop's History.
RenumberRenames the frames in order: F001, F002…

Select several

Ctrl + click adds a frame to the selection; Shift + click selects a range.

Drag drawings

  • Click a thumbnail and drag: a blue mark shows where it will go.
  • Works within the same row (reorder) and into another group's row.
  • Alt + drag copies instead of moving.
  • Dropping on a group's name sends the drawing to the end of that row.

Color labels

Select frames and click a color under Label. These are Photoshop's own layer colors, so they also show in the Layers panel. The group color in the header colors the group and all its frames at once.

Thumbnails

With Zoom to drawing on, thumbnails frame only the area where there is drawing in that group (the same for every frame). With it off, they show the whole page. The button rebuilds them all.

The buttons and the width and height controls make the timeline bigger or smaller.

5. Exposure and cut

Change the exposure

  • shorten or lengthen the exposure of the current frame (or the selected ones).
  • Or type the number in the exposure field: 24 puts the frame on x24 (up to x999).
  • Or stretch it in the timeline: hover over a cell's right edge (it turns blue) and drag sideways.

Default exposure

In default x, set the exposure for new frames (e.g. 2 to animate "on 2s"). Apply to all sets that exposure on every frame, or only on the selected ones.

Cut (razor)

Just like in video editing. Click (or press C) to turn the cut tool on; a red line shows where it will cut.

  • Click inside an exposure to split it: a drawing on x5 cut after its 3rd frame becomes x3 + x2. The right part is selected; press Delete to remove it.
  • Shift + click cuts and removes the rest at once: x5 becomes x3.
  • Esc turns the cut tool off.

6. Play, flip and onion skin

Play

plays the animation on the canvas itself, at the chosen fps, respecting the exposure. Next to the fps, choose Loop, Ping-pong (back and forth) or Once. Space or Esc stop it.

Flip

ButtonWhat it does
PreviousFlips from the previous drawings up to the current one.
AroundFrom the previous ones to the next ones, then back to the current one.
NextFrom the current one to the next ones, and back.
Orange bar / rulerClick and drag on the Flip bar or on the timeline's number ruler to pick a drawing.

The number next to the buttons sets how many drawings on each side.

Onion skin

  • Check Onion (or press O).
  • before / after: how many drawings show on each side, and each side's color.
  • opacity, tint (colors the lines) and fade (farther drawings get lighter).
  • Clear onion skin removes the tint and transparency from every frame. Use it before exporting or sending the file.

7. Pivot and mirror

Pivot & mirror tab. The pivot (anchor point) is the point the object rotates, scales and mirrors around, like in Toon Boom.

What the "object" is

  • With an active selection (lasso or marquee around the drawing): the object is what's selected.
  • Without a selection: the object is the whole layer selected in the Layers panel.

Set the pivot

  • 9 points: the little grid puts the pivot on the object's corners, edges or center.
  • From selection: pivot at the center of the selection. If the selection is tiny (a Lasso click), it only marks the point and disappears.
  • x / y: type the position in pixels. goes back to the center.
  • guides marks the pivot with two guides on the canvas.

Transform

ButtonWhat it does
Rotates on the pivot, by the chosen step in degrees.
By handOpens Photoshop's Ctrl+T with the reference point already on the pivot. Enter confirms.
Scales down or up from the pivot, by the chosen step in %.
ObjectMirrors the object horizontally or vertically, with the axis on the pivot.
ViewFlips the whole view (mirrored or upside down) to check the drawing. Press again to go back.
Mirrored drawingActivates the brush; click the butterfly in Photoshop's options bar and pick a symmetry. Whatever you draw on one side appears on the other.

8. Keys, Shift & Trace and cycles

Marks

Select frames and click K (key), B (breakdown) or I (inbetween). The letter shows in the timeline.

  • onion: keys only: the onion skin shows only keys and breakdowns.
  • pose test: playback shows only keys and breakdowns, each held until the next.

Shift & Trace

  1. On an inbetween, click (previous drawing) or (next).
  2. A tinted temporary copy of that drawing appears. Move it with the Move tool (V), Ctrl+T, the panel arrows, or rotate/mirror it with the pivot tab.
  3. Draw the inbetween over it and click Finish. The copy disappears; the original drawing is untouched.

Cycles

Repeats the selected frames (or all of them) as many times as you choose: loop, ping-pong (back and forth) or random (great for fire, water and boils).

9. Groups and light table

Use one [ANIM] group per working layer: rough, clean, character A, character B, animated background…

  • Each group is a row in the timeline. Click a row's name or one of its cells to edit it, at the same point in time.
  • renames the group; the color dot next to the list shows its color.
  • Groups tab > Sync all groups: play, flip and navigation show every group at the same time.
  • Light table: the other groups fade while you draw on the chosen group.

10. Guides and timing chart

Framing

Pick a format (whole document, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5). Guides appear for the format's border, the action safe area (93%) and the title safe area (90%).

Timing chart

Choose how many inbetweens, the easing (even, slow in, slow out or slow in & out), the direction and the size, then click Draw. The spacing chart is drawn on a separate [GUIA] layer, at the selected layer's pivot.

11. Export (PNG, GIF, MP4)

Export tab: pick the format and click Export…, then choose the folder. The export uses the whole image (with background), without onion skin, respecting exposure and synced groups.

FormatHow it works
PNG sequenceOne PNG per frame (name_0001.png…). Check one image per drawing to skip repeated exposures.
Animated GIFReady to share, looping, with each drawing's duration. In size, pick a percentage of the document (50% keeps the file light).
MP4 videoPegbar exports the PNG sequence and shows the steps below.

Make an MP4 in Photoshop

  1. File > Open and choose the first exported PNG (…_0001.png).
  2. Check Image Sequence, click Open and enter the same fps.
  3. File > Export > Render Video…, format H.264, and click Render.

You can also import the PNG sequence into editors such as Premiere, After Effects, DaVinci Resolve or CapCut.

13. Languages and "About"

In the right corner of the header, choose PT, EN or ES. The first time, Pegbar opens in your Photoshop's language. Buttons, descriptions, messages and the step names in Photoshop's History are translated.

(or the panel's menu) opens About Pegbar, with the version, the website and the link to support the project.

14. Troubleshooting

Thumbnails are blank

Make sure Zoom to drawing is checked and click Refresh. If it continues, open the Export tab and click Test thumbnails: a box with the details appears. Send us a screenshot of it.

Playback fills the History panel

In Preferences > History, uncheck "Make layer visibility changes undoable".

My drawings are tinted or transparent

That's the onion skin. Click Clear onion skin.

The pivot isn't where I marked it

Remember: with an active selection, the 9 points use the selection's bounds; without one, they use the layer. Turn on guides to see where the pivot is before rotating.

One of my layer styles disappeared

The onion skin tint uses the layer's "Color Overlay" effect. Avoid layer styles on drawing layers, or turn off tint in the onion skin.

I found a bug

Open an issue on GitHub or write to Coffee Cat Studio through the website, with a screenshot of the panel and your Photoshop version.
